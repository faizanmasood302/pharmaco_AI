# CPIC® Guideline for CYP2D6 and Opioid Therapy
**Source:** Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium (CPIC)
**Last Updated:** 2021 (Clinical Pharmacology & Therapeutics)

## 1. Overview
The CYP2D6 enzyme is responsible for the bioactivation of several opioid prodrugs into their more potent active metabolites. Genetic variation in the CYP2D6 gene significantly alters the therapeutic efficacy and safety profile of these medications.

## 2. Clinical Recommendations for Codeine & Tramadol
### 2.1 Ultra-Rapid Metabolizer (UM)
*   **Genotype:** Presence of >2 functional alleles (e.g., *1/*1xN, *1/*2xN).
*   **Biochemical Effect:** Accelerated conversion of codeine to morphine or tramadol to O-desmethyltramadol.
*   **Clinical Risk:** Increased risk of severe toxicity, including respiratory depression and fatality.
*   **Recommendation:** Avoid use of codeine or tramadol. Use an alternative analgesic not regulated by CYP2D6 (e.g., Morphine, Non-opioids).

### 2.2 Poor Metabolizer (PM)
*   **Genotype:** Presence of two non-functional alleles (e.g., *4/*4, *4/*5, *5/*6).
*   **Biochemical Effect:** Negligible conversion to active metabolites (Morphine/O-desmethyltramadol).
*   **Clinical Risk:** Subtherapeutic response, poor pain control, and risk of "dose-creep" leading to non-metabolic toxicity.
*   **Recommendation:** Avoid use of codeine or tramadol. Use an alternative analgesic not regulated by CYP2D6.

## 3. Clinical Recommendations for Oxycodone & Hydrocodone
*   **Mechanism:** CYP2D6 contributes to the O-demethylation of hydrocodone to hydromorphone and oxycodone to oxymorphone.
*   **Clinical Note:** While metabolism is affected, the parent compounds themselves possess significant analgesic activity.
*   **Guideline:** Monitor for reduced efficacy in PMs and increased side effects in UMs. CPIC Level: B/Informative.
