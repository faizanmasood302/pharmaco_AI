# CPIC Guideline for CYP2C19 and CYP2D6 and SSRI Therapy (2023 Update)

**Source:** Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium (CPIC)
**Authors:** Bousman CA, et al.
**Published:** Clinical Pharmacology & Therapeutics, 2023; 114(1):51-68
**PMID:** 37032427
**DOI:** 10.1002/cpt.2903

---

## 1. Overview

This guideline provides recommendations for the use of six SSRIs (citalopram, escitalopram, sertraline, paroxetine, fluvoxamine, and fluoxetine) based on CYP2C19 and CYP2D6 genotype. The guideline was developed through systematic literature review and evidence grading by the CPIC.

## 3. Therapeutic Recommendations

### 3.1 Citalopram (CYP2C19)

| Phenotype | Recommendation | Strength |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| **Ultra-rapid metabolizer** | Consider alternative not primarily metabolized by CYP2C19 (e.g., fluoxetine, paroxetine). If using citalopram, monitor for reduced efficacy. | Strong |
| **Rapid metabolizer** | Use label-recommended dosing (20-40 mg/day). | Strong |
| **Normal metabolizer** | Use label-recommended dosing (20-40 mg/day). | Strong |
| **Intermediate metabolizer** | Use label-recommended dosing. Monitor for side effects. | Strong |
| **Poor metabolizer** | **Reduce dose by 50%. Maximum 20 mg/day** due to dose-dependent QT prolongation risk. Consider alternative if tolerability issues. | Strong |

### 3.2 Escitalopram (CYP2C19)

| Phenotype | Recommendation | Strength |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| **Ultra-rapid metabolizer** | Consider alternative not primarily metabolized by CYP2C19 (e.g., fluoxetine). Monitor for reduced efficacy. | Strong |
| **Rapid metabolizer** | Use label-recommended dosing (10-20 mg/day). | Strong |
| **Normal metabolizer** | Use label-recommended dosing (10-20 mg/day). | Strong |
| **Intermediate metabolizer** | Use label-recommended dosing. Monitor for side effects. | Strong |
| **Poor metabolizer** | **Reduce dose by 50%** (e.g., 5-10 mg/day). Consider alternative if tolerability issues. | Strong |

### 3.3 Sertraline (CYP2C19)

| Phenotype | Recommendation | Strength |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| **Ultra-rapid metabolizer** | **No dose adjustment recommended.** Standard dosing (50-200 mg/day). | Moderate |
| **Rapid metabolizer** | Use label-recommended dosing (50-200 mg/day). | Moderate |
| **Normal metabolizer** | Use label-recommended dosing (50-200 mg/day). | Moderate |
| **Intermediate metabolizer** | Use label-recommended dosing. Monitor for side effects. | Moderate |
| **Poor metabolizer** | **Consider 50% dose reduction or alternative.** Monitor for increased side effects. | Moderate |

### 3.4 Paroxetine (CYP2D6)

| Phenotype | Recommendation | Strength |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| **Ultra-rapid metabolizer** | **Consider alternative not primarily metabolized by CYP2D6** (e.g., sertraline, fluoxetine). Avoid paroxetine if possible due to risk of therapeutic failure. | Strong |
| **Normal metabolizer** | Use label-recommended dosing (20-50 mg/day). | Strong |
| **Intermediate metabolizer** | Use label-recommended dosing. Monitor for side effects. | Strong |
| **Poor metabolizer** | **Reduce dose by 50%.** Consider alternative if tolerability issues. | Strong |

## 4. Safety Considerations

### QT Prolongation (Citalopram)

The FDA has issued a warning regarding dose-dependent QT interval prolongation with citalopram. In CYP2C19 poor metabolizers, citalopram concentrations are 2-3 fold higher, significantly increasing the risk of torsades de pointes. **Maximum recommended dose is 20 mg/day in CYP2C19 PMs.**

### Serotonin Syndrome

Ultra-rapid metabolizers receiving standard doses of paroxetine may have subtherapeutic concentrations, leading to dose escalation by clinicians and subsequent toxicity if the dose is increased beyond recommended limits. Monitor UMs closely.

### Pregnancy Considerations

SSRI pharmacokinetics change during pregnancy. CYP2C19 and CYP2D6 activity increases in the second and third trimesters, which may necessitate dose adjustments independent of genotype.

---

## 5. Evidence Summary

The CPIC guideline is based on:

- **50+ pharmacokinetic studies** demonstrating consistent relationships between CYP2C19/CYP2D6 genotype and SSRI exposure
- **18 clinical outcome studies** linking CYP2C19 poor metabolizer status with increased citalopram/escitalopram side effects
- **Meta-analyses** confirming reduced paroxetine efficacy in CYP2D6 ultra-rapid metabolizers
- **FDA label language** for citalopram warning about CYP2C19 PMs and QT prolongation
